## Why it's special

- **The water.** Lake Natron is fed by hot springs and rivers rich in sodium carbonate from the surrounding volcanic rock, and it has no outlet. As the water evaporates, it becomes hot, salty and caustic: often above 40 °C and up to 60 °C, with an alkalinity that can pass pH 12. It's less than 3 m deep, but in a wet year it covers up to about 1,000 km².
- **The colour.** Salt-loving microorganisms, among them cyanobacteria, thrive in the concentrated brine, and their red pigment turns the open water deep red and the salt crust pink and orange.
- **The flamingos.** Lake Natron is the only regular breeding site in East Africa for about 2.5 million lesser flamingos, and some 75% of the world's lesser flamingos are born here. The caustic mud keeps predators away from the nests.
- **The volcano.** Ol Doinyo Lengai, 2,962 m, is the only volcano known to erupt natrocarbonatite: lava at just 540–593 °C that flows like water, comes out black and turns white within hours to days. It erupted explosively in 2007.

The water's chemistry is known to calcify animals that die in it, which the photographer Nick Brandt made famous with his pictures of preserved birds and bats. The Lake Natron basin has been a Ramsar wetland of international importance since 2001.

## Getting there

Most visitors come from **Arusha**. The paved road takes you west to **Mto wa Mbu**, at the foot of the Rift Valley wall, about 113 km. After that the road is rough: it's about **120 km from Mto wa Mbu to Engaresero**, the Maasai village near the lake's south shore, past the village and ruins of Engaruka. Depending on the state of the road, that stretch can take from about 2.5 hours to 5–7 hours. Use a 4x4, travel in daylight, and carry water, food and spare fuel.

Some tracks in the area don't show on Google Maps: download an offline map that includes them, or go with a local driver. Most visitors book a vehicle, driver and guide through an operator in Arusha.

**Fees.** Area fees are collected at barriers on the roads into the Lake Natron area. Carry cash and keep the receipts.

## What to do

- **Walk out to the flamingos** with a local Maasai guide, across the mudflats near Engaresero. Keep your distance from the birds and stay off the nesting areas.
- **Climb Ol Doinyo Lengai.** The climb usually starts around midnight to reach the summit for sunrise, about six hours up on steep, loose ash. It's very tough: go only with an experienced local guide, a head torch, plenty of water and good boots, and check the volcano's current activity first.
- **See the salt and the colours** of the lake from the shore, best in the low light of early morning and late afternoon.

## When to go

The dry months are the easiest for the roads; heavy rain can make the tracks slow or impassable. The flamingos are present for much of the year, but the breeding season and the numbers vary with the water level. Allow at least two nights: it takes most of a day to get there and another to get back.

## Heat and safety

It gets very hot on the lake shore. Drink far more than you think you need, cover up from the sun, and never walk on the salt crust or into the water: it can burn skin and eyes. When we checked in October 2026, the UK government did not advise against travel to this part of Tanzania. Check your own government's advice before you go.
