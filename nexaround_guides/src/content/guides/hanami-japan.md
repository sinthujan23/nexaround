## When the trees bloom

The best-known cherry tree, the **Somei Yoshino**, flowers first in the south and last in the north. Average dates, from 30 years of observations (tenki.jp):

| City | First blossom | Full bloom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tokyo | about 24 March | about 3 April |
| Kyoto | about 26 March | about 4 April |
| Osaka | about 27 March | about 5 April |
| Sapporo | about 1 May | early May |

A tree reaches full bloom about a week to ten days after its first flowers open, and **peak blossom lasts roughly a week** before the petals fall. Wind and rain can end it sooner. Any single year can run a week early or late: **follow the forecasts**, which the Japan Weather Association (tenki.jp) and others publish from early in the year and update weekly.

If your dates are fixed, plan around the typical window and keep a flexible day or two; if they're not, wait for the first forecasts before booking. Travelling north later in April, to places like **Hirosaki**, can catch a second chance at full bloom.

## Where to go

- **Tokyo**: **Ueno Park** for the classic picnic scene under lanterns; **Chidorigafuchi**, along the Imperial Palace moat, for blossom over the water, with rowing boats and evening illuminations; **Shinjuku Gyoen**, a large garden with many varieties that flower at different times (entry fee; no alcohol, and bags are checked; check whether you need to book ahead in peak season).
- **Kyoto**: the **Philosopher's Path**, a canal-side walk lined with trees, and **Maruyama Park**, with its famous weeping cherry and evening picnics.
- **Osaka**: **Osaka Castle Park**, with blossom around the castle walls and moats.
- **The north**: **Hirosaki Park** in Aomori, with castle moats lined with trees, usually blooms in late April.

## Hanami etiquette

- **Don't touch, shake or pick the branches**, and don't climb the trees.
- **Take all your rubbish home**; bins are scarce and parks get overwhelmed.
- **Rules depend on the park**: some ban alcohol, music, barbecues or tarps left unattended. Read the signs at the entrance.
- Groups often send someone early to **reserve a spot** with a blue sheet. Don't take more space than you need, and take your shoes off before stepping onto someone's sheet.
- Popular spots are packed at weekends and in the evenings; go **early on a weekday morning** for the calmest views.

## Planning around the crowds

Cherry blossom season overlaps with the start of Japan's school and business year, and in late April with **Golden Week** holidays. Hotels in Tokyo and Kyoto fill up and prices rise: book early, and consider staying a little outside the centres.
