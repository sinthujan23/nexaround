## Read this first

The UK Foreign Office **advises against all travel on the Karakoram Highway between Mansehra and Chilas** via Battagram, Besham, Dasu and Sazin, up to the junction with the N15 (advice updated 13 October 2026). Our route goes round that stretch on the N15 through Naran and over the **Babusar Pass**, which is open in summer only, roughly June to September. Check your own government's advice before you go, and again just before you travel.

## Babusar Pass

The top of the pass on the N15 is the first big free stop: a broad saddle above the Kaghan valley, often in cloud. It's high and cold even in summer. Don't linger if you feel unwell from the altitude, and don't try it outside the season: the road is closed by snow for much of the year.

## Nanga Parbat from Thalichi

Back on the KKH beyond Chilas and past the Raikot Bridge, the road climbs to **Thalichi**, a village known for its roadside **Nanga Parbat viewpoint**. On a clear day you see the whole massif of **Nanga Parbat (8,126 m)**, the ninth-highest mountain on Earth, rising above the Indus. A hotel there is named after the view.

## The junction of three mountain ranges

About 20 km further on, near **Jaglot**, a monument by the road marks the place where three of the world's great ranges meet: the **Himalaya, the Karakoram and the Hindu Kush**. Just below, the **Gilgit River joins the Indus**. It's a roadside pull-out with a view. The road to Skardu branches off nearby.

## Rakaposhi View Point

North of Gilgit, in the village of **Ghulmet** in the Nagar valley, the KKH passes the **Rakaposhi View Point**, also called the "zero point": the closest view from the road of **Rakaposhi (7,788 m)**. Glacier-fed streams run past the viewpoint, and people sit with their feet in the cold water in summer. Stopping costs nothing; the cafés and tea stalls are there if you want them.

## The Sacred Rock of Hunza

Between Ganish and Attabad Lake, the **Sacred Rock of Hunza**, also called Haldeikish, sits beside the highway: a group of boulders about 180 m long, covered in carvings and inscriptions from the first millennium AD. Travellers on the old routes through the Hunza valley left their marks here in **Kharosthi, Brahmi, Sogdian, Chinese and Tibetan**, with ibex, hunters and stupas among the drawings, and the names of Kushan kings. It's a protected heritage site. Look, photograph, but don't touch, chalk or climb on the carvings.

## Attabad Lake

The turquoise lake that a landslide made in 2010 lies along the highway through a series of tunnels opened in 2015. The **viewpoints over the lake are free**. Boat trips and the lakeside resorts are not.

## Hussaini Suspension Bridge: a small toll

The famous swaying rope-and-plank bridge over the Hunza River at Hussaini is not free: there's a small toll to walk it, reported as about Rs 200. You can see it, and the Passu Cones behind it, from the roadside for nothing. The zip line next to it costs much more.

## The Passu Cones

Just north, at Passu, a marked roadside viewpoint looks across the river to the **Passu Cones**, also called the Passu Cathedral: a row of sharp, sawtooth peaks catching the light at sunrise and sunset. It's the classic photo of the upper Hunza valley, and it's free.
