## Why it's special

- **A green valley in a dry range.** The valley floor, between about 1,800 and 2,400 m and some 30 km long, is a patchwork of irrigated fields, orchards and poplars, framed by red and ochre mountains.
- **The villages.** Earth and stone houses cluster on the valley sides above the fields, in villages such as Tabant, Imelghas, Aït Imi and Zawyat Oulmzi. People here are Amazigh (Berber), and the houses, shared granaries and terraces show how a community lives with altitude and long winters.
- **Late to the road.** A paved road into the valley was completed only around 2000. Before that, for much of the year, the valley was reached on foot and by mule.
- **Big geology.** The valley is part of the **M'Goun Geopark**, which joined UNESCO's network of geoparks in 2014. The M'Goun itself, at 4,071 m, is one of the highest peaks in the Atlas, and there are dinosaur footprints in the rocks nearby.

## Getting there

| Leg | Distance | Driving |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marrakesh → Azilal | 167 km | about 2 h 51 |
| Azilal → Tabant | 73 km | about 1 h 41 |

The usual route is from **Marrakesh to Azilal**, then up into the mountains to **Tabant**, the valley's main village. You can also come in from Demnate. The road beyond Azilal climbs over passes with steep bends and long drops; drive it in daylight. Without a car, take a bus or shared taxi to Azilal and arrange onward transport with your guesthouse, or come with a trekking operator from Marrakesh.

The Ouzoud Waterfalls are a popular detour on the way to Azilal.

## What to do

- **Walk the valley.** Paths and tracks link the villages along both sides of the valley: one of the best ways to spend a few days is simply walking from village to village, staying in a different guesthouse each night.
- **Climb the hill of Sidi Moussa.** The round building on the hilltop above Tabant is the shrine of Sidi Moussa, and the view over the whole valley is the best there is.
- **Find the dinosaur footprints.** Ask in the valley or at the M'Goun Geopark House in Azilal where to see them, and go with a local guide.
- **Trek towards the M'Goun.** The valley is a starting point for treks into the M'Goun massif. Go with a licensed mountain guide and mules, and allow several days.
- **The Tabant souk.** The weekly market brings people in from all over the valley.

## When to go

- **Spring (April to June)** is green, with the fields full and the mountains still snowy.
- **Autumn (September to November)** brings the harvest and clear, settled weather.
- **Summer** is the best time for high treks, but the valley floor can be hot in the day.
- **Winter** is cold, with snow on the passes and freezing nights. Check road conditions before you drive in.

## Where to stay

The valley has small family guesthouses (gîtes) in many of its villages, most serving dinner and breakfast. Book ahead in spring, especially for weekends and holidays.

## Visiting with respect

This is a conservative, rural community. Dress modestly, ask before photographing people, especially women, and before entering any home, granary or shrine. Keep to paths through the fields: every patch of green is someone's harvest.
