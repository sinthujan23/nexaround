  In the secluded valleys of the Ryōhaku Mountains between Gifu and Toyama, the village of Ogimachi in Shirakawa-gō is famous for its collection of gasshō-zukuri farmhouses. Gasshō-zukuri translates to "constructed like hands in prayer"—a reference to the steep, 60-degree thatched roofs designed to shed the massive snowdrifts of Japanese winter.

## Visiting Shirakawa-gō

- **How to reach:** Highway express buses operated by Nohi Bus run frequently between Takayama (50 mins) and Kanazawa (75 mins). Seat reservations are mandatory during peak seasons and should be booked one month in advance.
- **Staying in a Minshuku:** A handful of gasshō-zukuri homes operate as family-run guesthouses (*minshuku*). Guests sleep on tatami mats, eat local Hida beef and mountain vegetables cooked around the charcoal *irori*, and experience the village after the day-tripper tour buses leave.
- **Winter Light-Up:** On select Sunday nights in January and February, the snow-covered houses are illuminated with warm floodlights. To control overcrowding, attendance is strictly restricted to lottery ticket holders.
