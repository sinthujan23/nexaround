## What it is

The festival honours the **Virgen de la Candelaria**, Puno's patron saint, and mixes Catholic devotion with the music, dance and symbolism of the Andes. Its main bearers are the **Quechua and Aymara** people of the Puno region, and its dances range from the **native dances** of rural communities, with panpipes and drums, to the **costume dances** of the city's neighbourhoods: the **Diablada**, with its huge devil masks; the **Morenada**, with its kings and swaying skirts; the Caporales and many more.

More than **200 dances** are performed by over **150 groups**, organised by the regional folklore federation. Behind them are thousands of mask-makers, embroiderers and instrument-makers who work on the costumes all year.

## When it happens

The feast day is **2 February**, and the festival fills roughly the **first half of February**. It starts with masses and novenas in the days before, and includes:

- **The feast day** itself, with mass and a procession of the Virgin's image through the city.
- **Dawn serenades** (*albas*) by the bands, including on **Huajsapata hill** above the city.
- **The dance contests** in the **Enrique Torres Belón stadium**, where native and costume groups compete. You need a ticket.
- **The parade of veneration**, when the costume groups dance through the streets of Puno for hours on end.

Exact dates change each year: check the programme from the **Federación Regional de Folklore y Cultura de Puno** or the Puno municipality before you book.

## The altitude

Puno sits at about **3,830 m**. If you fly in from the coast, you'll feel it.

- **Take it slowly** for the first two days: walk slowly, drink plenty of water, go easy on alcohol, and eat light.
- If you can, **acclimatise on the way**, for example in Arequipa or Cusco.
- Headache and breathlessness are common at first; if you get confused, very breathless at rest or can't keep down fluids, get medical help and go down.

## Practical notes

- **Fly into Juliaca**, 48 km away, about 70 minutes by road. Buses also run from Cusco and Arequipa.
- **Book your room early**: hotels fill and prices rise for the festival.
- February is the **rainy season** on the altiplano, and nights are cold. Bring a rain jacket and warm layers.
- **Streets are packed** on the main days. Keep valuables close and expect long hours of music and dancing: some groups dance late into the night.
- **The UK Foreign Office** doesn't advise against travel to Puno. Protests and road blockades sometimes hit the region, so check the news before you travel.
