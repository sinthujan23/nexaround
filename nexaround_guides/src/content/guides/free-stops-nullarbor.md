## Penong: windmills and a pink lake

**Penong Windmill Museum.** Right beside the highway in Penong, 73 km west of Ceduna, volunteers have restored and re-erected a field of old farm windmills. The display opened in 2016, and its centrepiece is a huge Comet windmill nicknamed "Bruce", said to be the biggest in Australia. It's an open-air display: pull in, walk around, take the photo.

**Lake MacDonnell.** About 13 km south of Penong on the Point Sinclair road, a causeway crosses a salt lake that turns pink, coloured by salt and algae. The same road carries on to Cactus Beach, a well-known surf break.

## Head of Bight: worth paying for

Not free, but if you cross between May and October it's the one stop to pay for. Southern right whales come into the Head of Bight to calve, and a boardwalk and platform look straight down on them from the cliffs. It sits within the Yalata Indigenous Protected Area, about 20 km east of the Nullarbor Roadhouse. August is usually the best month. There's an entry fee.

## Bunda Cliffs lookouts

West of the Nullarbor Roadhouse the highway runs close to the edge of the continent, where the Bunda Cliffs drop more than 120 m into the Southern Ocean. There are three designated clifftop lookouts off the highway. Use only those: the cliff edge is unstable and collapses happen without warning. Camping along the cliffs is not allowed.

## Border Village: Rooey II

At the WA border, a giant kangaroo called Rooey II stands outside the roadhouse, can in hand. It's the obvious photo of the crossing. While you're there: this is where Western Australia's quarantine check takes your fresh fruit, vegetables and honey, so eat them first.

## Eucla: ruins in the dunes, and a jetty

**Eucla Telegraph Station.** A few kilometres south of Eucla, down a track towards the sea, stand the stone walls of a telegraph station opened in 1877. The dunes have been slowly swallowing it for decades.

**Eucla Jetty.** Carry on to the beach and the old timber jetty runs out over white sand and turquoise water. It's one of the most photogenic stops on the whole crossing, and almost nobody is there.

## Madura Pass lookout

At Madura the highway drops down an escarpment onto the Roe Plains. The lookout beside the pass gives a wide view over the plain and the road running out across it. Good at sunrise and sunset.

## Eyre Bird Observatory (detour, small fee)

About 50 km from Cocklebiddy, on the coast, BirdLife Australia runs a bird observatory in an old telegraph station. Day visitors are welcome but must book, and the last stretch of track is four-wheel drive only; the caretakers can arrange to meet you if you don't have one. There's a small entry fee.

## Caiguna Blowhole

About 5 km west of the Caiguna roadhouse, a hole in the limestone breathes. Depending on the air pressure you can feel air rushing out of the ground, or being sucked in. Watch your footing around the edge.

## The 90 Mile Straight

Between Caiguna and Balladonia the Eyre Highway runs 146.6 km without a single bend: Australia's longest straight road. There's a sign at each end, and stopping for a photo at one of them is a Nullarbor ritual. Pull fully off the road to do it; road trains don't slow down.

## Balladonia

The roadhouse at Balladonia has a small museum with pieces of the Skylab space station, which broke up over this area in 1979, along with displays on local history.

## Sleeping for free: rest areas

Main Roads WA runs rest areas along the Eyre Highway. Those signed as **24-hour rest areas** let you stop for up to 24 consecutive hours; they are meant for resting, not as campgrounds, so arrive late, leave early and take everything with you. On the South Australian side, there's no camping along the Bunda Cliffs, and inside the Nullarbor Wilderness Protection Area camping is only at Koonalda Homestead, booked in advance.

Read every sign: rules differ from one rest area to the next.
