## The loop

From **Búðardalur** in the Dalir district, the loop runs north along the Strandir coast to **Hólmavík**, then west round fjord after fjord to **Ísafjörður**, the region's main town. From there it turns south through the **Dýrafjarðargöng tunnel** (opened in 2020, linking the north and south of the region) past **Þingeyri** to **Dynjandi**, then over to **Bíldudalur** and **Patreksfjörður**, and back east along the south coast through **Flókalundur** and **Reykhólar**.

Our measured loop is **734 km**. The official Westfjords Way, at about **950 km**, adds more detours; a quarter of it is gravel.

Distances look short on the map but fjord roads are slow: every fjord means driving all the way in and all the way out again. Allow at least five days, ideally a week.

## Detours worth taking

- **Látrabjarg:** the longest bird cliff in the North Atlantic, at **Iceland's westernmost point**. In summer, puffins nest along the cliff edge. It's 60 km from Patreksfjörður, the last stretch on gravel. Stay well back from the edge: it's undercut and crumbling, with few or no fences.
- **Rauðisandur:** a vast beach of red-gold sand, reached by a steep, narrow gravel road with hairpins. There's a little black church near the bottom.
- **Hornstrandir:** the roadless, uninhabited far north of the region, a nature reserve where **Arctic foxes** live at the highest density anywhere. Reach it by boat from Ísafjörður or Bolungarvík in summer, and hike it with proper gear.
- **Geothermal pools:** small natural hot pools are dotted around the region; ask locally which are open to visitors.

## Ferries

The **Baldur car ferry** crosses Breiðafjörður between **Stykkishólmur** (on the Snæfellsnes peninsula) and **Brjánslækur**, near Flókalundur. It saves a long drive and makes a good one-way entry or exit. Book ahead for a car in summer.

## When to go

**June to early September.** In summer you get long days, puffins at Látrabjarg, boats to Hornstrandir and every road open. Outside summer, snow and ice close some mountain roads for months, and weather can shut others at short notice. Check the official road-conditions site (**umferdin.is**) every morning, at any time of year.

## Driving tips

- **Fuel** is available in the main villages, but stations are far apart on the south coast. Fill up whenever you can.
- **Single-lane bridges and tunnels** are common: the car closest to the bridge or the passing place has priority.
- **Gravel roads:** slow down where the asphalt ends. Loose gravel at speed is a common cause of accidents for visitors.
- **Off-road driving is illegal in Iceland**, and heavily fined.
