## Why it's special

- **A town on the water.** Ganvié is not a few huts on stilts but a whole town, with homes, shops, schools, restaurants and a floating market, and about 20,000 people. The only way in or out is by boat.
- **Its history.** The Tofinu moved onto the lake in the 16th or 17th century, beyond the reach of the warriors who were raiding the country for captives to sell to European slave traders. The lake became their refuge, and they never left.
- **Life on the lake.** Ganvié was once a farming community; today, besides tourism, most people make their living from fishing and fish farming. People are often called "water men", and Ganvié is often called the "Venice of Africa".

Ganvié has been on UNESCO's World Heritage tentative list since 1996.

## Getting there

From **Cotonou**, it's about **18 km by road to the pier at Abomey-Calavi**, on the shore of Lake Nokoué: 30 to 45 minutes depending on traffic. Boats leave from the pier, and the crossing to the village takes about 35 minutes.

**Boats and prices.** You can join a boat with other visitors or charter one with a guide. In 2026, reports from the pier gave prices from about 3,000 CFA francs on a shared commercial boat to 5,000–15,000 CFA francs per person for a guided tour, and around 20,000 CFA francs for a private group boat. Prices are negotiable: agree on the price, the length of the tour and what it includes **before** you get in.

**A guide** is worth having: the waterways are a maze, many residents don't speak English, and a local guide can explain the village and help you visit respectfully.

## What a visit is like

Most visits last 3 to 4 hours. You'll glide along the main channels past homes and shops on stilts, see the floating market where women sell from their canoes, and stop at a restaurant or a craft shop on stilts. Morning and late afternoon have the best light and the coolest air.

## When to go

The dry season, from about November to March, is the most comfortable time to visit. It's hot and humid all year.

## Visiting with respect

This is people's home, and many residents don't want to be photographed by passing tourists. **Always ask before you take a photo of anyone,** accept a no, and don't photograph children without their parents' permission. Buying something in the market or eating in a village restaurant puts money directly into the community.

## Safety

The areas where the UK government advises against travel are all in Benin's far north, far from Ganvié and Cotonou. As anywhere, keep valuables out of sight in Cotonou, and in the boat keep your phone and camera in a dry bag.
