## What Vodun is

Vodun is the traditional religion of the Fon, Ewe and neighbouring peoples of Benin and Togo. It recognises a supreme creator and many *vodun*, spirits and deities linked to nature, ancestors and the forces of life, such as Sakpata, Heviosso (thunder), Mami Wata (the sea) and Zo (fire). It travelled across the Atlantic with enslaved people and grew into Haitian Vodou, Brazilian Candomblé and other religions of the Americas.

Benin honours it with a **national holiday for traditional religions** in January. Since 2024 the government has also run the **Vodun Days** in Ouidah, a festival of ceremonies, music and culture that draws followers from across Benin and West Africa, members of the diaspora, and visitors.

## When it happens

The 2027 Vodun Days run from **2 to 9 January** in Ouidah. It's the first edition to last a week instead of three days. The opening days are cultural events and concerts; the big ritual ceremonies are planned for the **final weekend**. Thursday 7 and Friday 8 January 2027 are public holidays in Benin.

## What you'll see

- **Zangbeto**, the "guardians of the night": tall cones of brightly dyed raffia that spin and glide with no one visible inside. In Vodun belief they keep order in the community.
- **Egungun**, the returning ancestors: masked figures covered from head to foot in layers of embroidered and sequined cloth. By tradition they must not be touched, so give them room when they pass.
- **Drums, songs and processions** of followers of the different vodun, many dressed in white or in the colours of their deity.
- **Concerts on Ouidah beach**, which opened the 2026 festival, and ceremonies at the **Door of No Return**.

## Ouidah beyond the festival

- **The Slave Route** (*Route des Esclaves*) runs about 4 km from the town to the beach, past memorials to the enslaved people marched to the ships. At its end stands the **Door of No Return**, a memorial arch facing the ocean.
- **The Python Temple**, in the centre of town, is a working Vodun temple where royal pythons live. There's a small entry fee.
- **The Sacred Forest of Kpassè** is a grove of old trees and bronze statues of Vodun deities. There's a small fee.

## Visiting with respect

- **This is a religion, not a show.** Dress modestly, stay calm and give processions and masked figures space.
- **Ask before photographing people**, and accept a no. Some shrines and ceremonies don't allow photos at all; at others, a fee is asked for a camera. Follow what you're told.
- **Don't touch masks, costumes or sacred objects.**
- **A local guide** from Ouidah helps a lot: they can tell you which ceremony is which, where you may stand, and who to ask.

## Getting there and staying

Fly into **Cotonou**; Ouidah is **42 km** away, about an hour by road. Rooms in Ouidah are few and fill up for the festival: book early, or stay in Cotonou and travel each day. Shared taxis and motorbike taxis (*zémidjans*) run between the two.

## Safety

The areas where the UK government advises against travel are in Benin's far north, far from Ouidah. Crowds are big on the main days: keep your phone and money out of sight, and drink plenty of water, as January is hot and dry on the coast.
