## Why it's special

- **One road, part of the year.** The road from Pshaveli climbs over the Abano Pass at 2,826 m, one of the highest drivable passes in the Caucasus, then drops into Tusheti. Snow usually closes it from about mid-October to mid-June, and its rough surface allows only 4x4s.
- **Summer villages.** Tush shepherds spend the summer in the highlands and the winter in the lowland villages of Zemo and Kvemo Alvani. Many villages are almost empty for half the year.
- **Towers.** Stone defence towers and slate-roofed houses stand above the valleys, at Omalo's Keselo fortress, at Dartlo and in villages up and down the region.
- **Wild country.** Tusheti National Park, set up in 2003, covers about 83,453 ha, with mountains rising to over 4,000 m.

Tusheti as a whole covers 969 km². Omalo, at about 1,880 m, is its largest village and the base for most visitors.

## The road over the Abano Pass

| Leg | Distance | Driving |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tbilisi → Telavi | 93 km | about 1 h 44 |
| Telavi → Pshaveli | 32 km | about 35 min |
| Pshaveli → Abano Pass | 40 km | about 1 h 33 |
| Abano Pass → Omalo | 28 km | about 1 h 17 |

From Pshaveli to Omalo is **68 km** by our measure (72 km by the Roads Department's). The road climbs in tight bends up a steep valley, crosses the pass, and drops down the other side on a ledge above long drops. It is narrow, often muddy, and in places the surface is washed out. Drivers who know the road do it every day in summer. If you haven't driven a mountain track like this before, don't make this your first.

**Road works.** In 2026 the Roads Department of Georgia was upgrading the Pshaveli–Abano–Omalo road, with work on about 49 km of it planned in phases through 2026 and 2027. Expect delays, and check the road's status before you set out.

## Getting there

- **Shared 4x4.** In season, shared 4x4s leave from the main junction in Kvemo Alvani, timed to meet the minibuses from Tbilisi. A 2026 guide put the fare at around 100 GEL per person and the drive at about 5 hours: far longer than Google's estimate. A few also leave from Telavi bus station.
- **Private 4x4 with a driver.** The easiest way, especially in a group. Ask your guesthouse in Omalo to arrange one.
- **Your own car.** Only in a proper 4x4 with good tyres, only in dry weather, and check first that your rental contract allows it.

## What to do

- **Omalo and the Keselo fortress.** Climb to the towers above Upper Omalo for the view over the valleys.
- **Dartlo,** north of Omalo: a village of stone towers and slate-roofed houses above the river.
- **Hike.** Trails link the villages and climb to high passes. Get advice and maps at the Tusheti Protected Areas visitor centre in Omalo, and ask whether your route needs a border-zone permit.
- **Eat Tush food,** such as the local guda cheese, in your guesthouse.

## When to go

- **July and August** are the most reliable months: the road is open, the guesthouses are busy, and the shepherds are up with their flocks.
- **June and September** can be beautiful but the pass may open late or close early. Check before you go.
- **October to May,** the road is usually closed by snow.

## Visiting with respect

Tusheti has strong customs of its own, including shrines that visitors should not enter. Ask your hosts what is fine to visit and to photograph, keep to the paths across hay meadows, and leave no rubbish: everything has to be driven out over the pass.
