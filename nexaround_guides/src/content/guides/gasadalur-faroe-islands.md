## Why it's special

- **The waterfall.** Múlafossur pours off the edge of the village's green shelf and falls straight into the sea. On a clear day, with the island of Mykines behind, it's one of the best-known views in the Faroe Islands.
- **Almost cut off for centuries.** Until 2004 there was no road. People walked over the mountain from Bøur, on a path more than 400 m high, and carried everything with them. Boats had to be kept at Bøur, because Gásadalur had no safe landing. A stairway from the beach up to the village was only built in 1940.
- **Tiny.** Gásadalur had 23 people at its peak in 2010, 11 in 2020, and 16 in 2025.
- **The setting.** The village sits in a valley under Árnafjall, at 722 m the highest mountain on Vágar, looking west over the fjord to Mykines.

## Getting there

Fly into **Vágar Airport**, the Faroe Islands' only airport, or drive over from Tórshavn through the subsea tunnel **Vágatunnilin**, about 4.9 km long. The tunnel has a road toll.

| Leg | Distance | Driving |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tórshavn → Vágar Airport | 46 km | about 45 min |
| Vágar Airport → Gásadalur | 11 km | about 18 min |

From the airport, the road runs through Sørvágur and Bøur, then into the tunnel to Gásadalur. There's no public transport to Bøur or Gásadalur, so come by car, taxi or tour.

## The old path over the mountain

Before the tunnel, the path from Bøur over the mountain was the village's only link by land, used by everyone, the postman included. Today it's a hike of about 5 km each way with a climb to around 400 m. Allow at least half a day there and back. The path is marked, but it is steep in places and hard to follow in cloud: don't set out in poor visibility or bad weather. Visit Faroe Islands has the route and the current access information.

## At the viewpoint

The path to the Múlafossur viewpoint is short and easy, a few minutes from the village. The cliffs around it are very steep and the edges are unstable. **Stay on the marked path and outside the fences,** however good the photo would be from the other side.

## When to go

- **May to September** brings the longest days and the mildest weather, and the best chance of a clear view to Mykines.
- **Any time of year,** fog and rain can roll in within minutes. The waterfall is often at its most impressive after rain.
- **Winter** brings short days, gales and occasional snow, which can make the scene even more dramatic, if you can reach it.

## Visiting with care

Sixteen people live here, and many thousands of visitors come to look. Park only where parking is marked, don't walk through gardens or fields, keep away from the sheep, and keep your voice down in the village. Gásadalur is someone's home first and a viewpoint second.
