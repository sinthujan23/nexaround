## Why it's special

- **No road.** Supai is reached on foot, on horseback, or by helicopter. The trail from Hualapai Hilltop drops about 2,000 ft (600 m) in 8 miles (13 km).
- **Mule mail.** Supai is the only place in the United States where the mail still goes in and out by mule. A contractor drives it to the trailhead and the mules carry it down the canyon, up to 200 pounds each, along with groceries and parcels for the village.
- **The water.** Havasu Creek runs a startling blue-green over red rock, and below the village it falls in a chain of waterfalls: Havasu, Mooney and Beaver Falls among them.
- **The people.** Supai is the home of the Havasupai Tribe. In 2023 the Associated Press reported that about 500 of the Tribe's nearly 770 members live in the village. This is not a national park: the Tribe runs the land, the visits and the rules.

## Permits: the first step

You can't just turn up. Every visit needs a reservation from the Havasupai Tribe, bought through the Tribe's official site, havasupaireservations.com:

- Stays are **three nights**, at the campground or the lodge. **No day hiking is allowed.**
- In 2026, a campground permit cost **$455 per person** for three nights, and the lodge **$2,277 for up to four people** for three nights.
- Reservations for the year go on sale early in the year. In 2026, early access ran from 21 to 31 January and the regular sale opened on 1 February. Demand is far higher than the number of places.

Buy only from the official site. The Tribe's rules apply on its land and can change without notice.

## Getting there

**Drive to Hualapai Hilltop.** From Interstate 40 at Kingman, take Route 66 to Peach Springs, then Indian Route 18 to its end at the rim. There are no shops or fuel at the Hilltop, so arrive with a full tank and everything you need for the hike.

| Leg | Distance | Driving |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kingman → Peach Springs | 81 km (50 mi) | about 54 min |
| Peach Springs → Hualapai Hilltop | 109 km (68 mi) | about 1 h 16 |

**Hike down.** From the Hilltop it's **8 miles (13 km) to Supai** and **10 miles (16 km) to the campground**, which is 2 miles past the village. Mooney Falls is another half mile beyond the campground. The trail starts with steep switchbacks, then follows a dry, rocky wash into the canyon. Start at first light: the canyon gets very hot, and the trails are closed when the temperature passes 115 °F (46 °C).

**Helicopter.** A helicopter flies between the Hilltop and the village on a limited schedule. Don't count on it to get you in or out: priority and timetables are set by the Tribe.

## In the canyon

- **Havasu Falls** is the one in the photographs: a single fall into a turquoise pool, between the village and the campground.
- **Mooney Falls** is taller and is reached by a steep descent through tunnels and down chains and ladders on wet rock. Take it slowly.
- **Beaver Falls** is further down the creek. Floods in 2018, 2019, 2022 and 2024 have damaged parts of the canyon and some areas are closed: check before you go, and respect every closure sign.

## Safety

- There are **no public medical facilities** in Supai. An injury can take hours or even days to reach treatment.
- **Flash floods** can fill the canyon fast. In 2018, 200 visitors were evacuated by helicopter. If you hear of storms upstream, get to high ground and follow instructions from the Tribe.
- **Heat** is the most common danger on the trail. Carry plenty of water and start early.

## Visiting as a guest

Supai is people's home, not a resort. Stay on the trails, keep out of homes and yards, ask before photographing anyone, and follow every rule the Tribe sets. Your permit fees go to the Tribe.
