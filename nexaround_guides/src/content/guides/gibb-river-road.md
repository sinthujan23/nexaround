## The route

The Gibb runs roughly east–west across the Kimberley, about 660 km from **Derby** to the **Great Northern Highway** between Kununurra and Wyndham. We measured Derby to Kununurra at **720 km**.

- **Derby to Imintji:** the western end, with the big gorges of the King Leopold Ranges (Windjana, Bell, Lennard, Galvans) on short side roads.
- **Imintji to Mount Barnett:** the station country, with Manning Gorge behind the Mount Barnett Roadhouse.
- **Mount Barnett to the Pentecost:** the longest, emptiest stretch. The Kalumburu Road turns north to Drysdale River Station and the remote Mitchell Plateau.
- **The Pentecost to Kununurra:** the famous river crossing under the Cockburn Ranges, then El Questro and Emma Gorge, and back onto bitumen.

## Road and vehicle

Since the mid-2000s the Gibb has been a **formed gravel road**, with a few short sealed sections. **A 4WD is strongly recommended**: river crossings and heavy corrugations break ordinary cars and their tyres. Before you go:

- Carry **two full-size spare tyres**, a puncture repair kit and a compressor. Lower your tyre pressures on corrugations.
- Pack **recovery gear**, water, food for extra days, and a satellite messenger or beacon. There's little or no phone signal.
- **Check road status before each section.** Roads close after rain, even outside the wet season. The status boards at junctions and **Main Roads WA's road-conditions site** are the authority.

## The season

The Gibb is **only open in the dry season**, roughly **May to October**, and only after it has been graded and declared open. In the wet, typically **November to March** or later, floods close it, sometimes for months. Early in the dry (May–June), the road is usually freshly graded and the waterfalls still flow; by September the corrugations are usually at their worst and the heat is building.

## Fuel

Fuel stops are few, and **not all of them sell petrol**:

- **Imintji**: store and campground; check what fuel it has before relying on it.
- **Mount Barnett Roadhouse**: petrol and diesel.
- **Drysdale River Station**: about 60 km north up the Kalumburu Road.
- **El Questro**: near the eastern end.

The longest gap, **Mount Barnett to El Questro, is about 330 km**, more once you add side trips to the gorges. Carry extra fuel, especially in a petrol vehicle.

## Gorges worth the detours

- **Windjana Gorge**: a gorge cut through a fossil reef, where freshwater crocodiles sun on the banks.
- **Bell Gorge**: a waterfall dropping into a deep swimming hole, a short walk from the car park.
- **Manning Gorge**: swim (or float your bag) across the river at Mount Barnett, then walk to the falls.
- **El Questro and Emma Gorge**: gorges, hot springs and a tall, narrow plunge pool, inside a private wilderness park that needs a permit.

National park gorges charge an entry fee, and station properties charge for camping and access.

## Crocodiles

**Saltwater crocodiles live in the Pentecost and other big rivers.** Never wade across a river to check its depth, never swim where you're not sure it's safe, and camp well back from the water. Freshwater crocodiles, like those at Windjana, are shy, but leave them alone.

## Where to sleep

**Cattle stations** along the road offer camping, cabins and meals: Mount Hart, Mount Elizabeth, Ellenbrae, Drysdale River, Home Valley, El Questro and others, some well off the main road. There are also bush camps at the national park gorges. In peak season, book ahead.
