## Structure of the Perahera

The festival progresses across three distinct phases:

1. **Kumbal Perahera (Nights 1 to 5):**
   The opening series of smaller, quieter processions designed to ward off misfortune and bring rains. Elephants wear simpler fabrics, and crowds are lighter.
2. **Randoli Perahera (Nights 6 to 10):**
   The grand processions. *Randoli* refers to the palanquins in which the consorts of the deities used to travel. On the final night, the Maligawa Tusker carries the sacred relic casket on its back, stepping on a white carpet (*pawada*) unrolled ahead of its feet.
3. **Diya Kepeema (Water Cutting):**
   On the morning following the final night procession, the priests travel to the Mahaweli River at Getambe to perform the water-cutting ceremony, cleaving the river with a golden sword and collecting water in clay vessels for the temple year.

## Tips for visitors

- **Street vs paid seats:** Shops, restaurants, and hotels along Dalada Veediya and D.S. Senanayake Street set up folding chairs on their verandas for \$30 to \$80 USD. Alternatively, reserve a spot on the public pavement curb by laying down newspapers by 14:00.
- **Dress Code:** Shoulders and knees must be respectfully covered. White clothing is traditional and warmly appreciated by locals.
- **Transport:** Trains from Colombo to Kandy book out weeks in advance for Perahera season. Book your Observation Car or 2nd Class reserved seat as soon as tickets open 30 days prior.
