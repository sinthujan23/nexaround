## Why it's special

- **A Balti village in India.** Turtuk and its four neighbouring villages, Bogdang, Tyakshi, Chalunkha and Thang, are the only places in India where the Balti people live. They speak Balti, Ladakhi and Urdu.
- **It changed countries.** Until 1971, Turtuk was under Pakistani control as part of Baltistan. In the war that year, the Indian Army took it, and the village has been in India ever since. Families were split across the new line.
- **It's new to visitors.** Turtuk opened to tourists only in 2010. In the 2011 census it had 3,371 people in 384 households.
- **Apricots.** Turtuk is known for its fruit, above all its apricots, which you'll see drying on rooftops in late summer.

## Getting there

**Fly or drive to Leh** first, and spend a few days there to acclimatise. Leh is at about 3,500 m, and the road to Turtuk climbs much higher.

**Then drive north over Khardung La.** The road climbs to the pass, drops into the Shyok valley, passes Diskit and Hunder in the Nubra Valley, and then follows the Shyok west towards the Line of Control.

| Leg | Distance | Driving |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leh → Khardung La | 39 km | about 1 h 12 |
| Khardung La → Khalsar | 54 km | about 1 h 16 |
| Khalsar → Diskit | 21 km | about 30 min |
| Diskit → Hunder | 12 km | about 24 min |
| Hunder → Turtuk | 85 km | about 2 h 12 |

That's **211 km and about 5 h 30 of driving** without stops. Most people break the trip with a night in Diskit or Hunder, and that also helps with the altitude. Shared taxis and buses run from Leh to Nubra; many visitors hire a car and driver for the whole trip.

**Khardung La** is signposted as 5,602 m (18,380 ft), the "highest motorable road in the world". Surveys put it at 5,359 m (17,582 ft): still extremely high. Don't stay long at the top, and come back down if you get a headache, dizziness or nausea.

## Permits

- **Indian nationals** have not needed an Inner Line Permit for Nubra since August 2021, but visitors still pay an environment fee. Pay it online before you travel and carry the receipt.
- **Foreign nationals** need a **Protected Area Permit** for Nubra, applied for through a registered travel agent in Leh or the official online portal. Rules for Turtuk and the villages beyond it have changed several times, and in 2026 Ladakh was considering a single permit for the whole territory. Check the current rules before you go.

Carry your permit, passport or ID: they're checked at army posts on the way.

## What to do

- **Walk the village.** Turtuk is split in two by a stream, and paths lead between orchards, water channels and stone houses.
- **The Balti Heritage Museum,** a small museum of village life, and the **old royal house** of Turtuk's former rulers.
- **Look across the valley.** From the upper village there are wide views up and down the Shyok valley.
- **On the way:** Diskit Monastery, the oldest and largest in Nubra, with its giant statue of Maitreya, and the sand dunes and Bactrian camels at Hunder.

## When to go

- **May to September** is the main season, when the road over Khardung La is most reliable and homestays are open. The pass has no permanent winter closure, but snow and landslides cause long delays.
- **Spring** brings apricot blossom; **late summer** brings the harvest.
- **Winter** is very cold, and most visitors stay away.

## Visiting with respect

Turtuk is a conservative Muslim village. Dress modestly, with shoulders and knees covered, ask before photographing anyone, and above all women, and don't wander into fields, orchards or homes uninvited. Stay in a local homestay if you can: it's the best way to meet people, and the money stays in the village.
