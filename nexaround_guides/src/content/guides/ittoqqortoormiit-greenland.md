  On the rugged eastern coastline of Greenland, perched on the mouth of Scoresby Sound—the largest and longest fjord system on Earth—lies Ittoqqortoormiit. With approximately 350 inhabitants, it is widely considered one of the most isolated permanent communities on the planet.

## The journey to the end of the line

Reaching Ittoqqortoormiit requires careful logistical coordination:

1. Fly from Reykjavík Domestic Airport (RKV) or Akureyri (AEY) in Iceland to Nerlerit Inaat (Constable Point Airport, CNP) aboard an Icelandair or Norlandair Dash-8.
2. From Nerlerit Inaat, board an Air Greenland Bell 212 helicopter for the 15-minute hop across the ice of Hurry Inlet to the village heliport.
3. In summer (August to September), two supply ships arrive carrying food, machinery, and timber for the upcoming winter. In winter, dog sleds and snowmobiles cross the sea ice.

## Life in the Arctic

- **Subsistence traditions:** Most local families still live through traditional Inuit subsistence hunting. Sled dogs (*Qimmeq*) outnumber human residents, chained outside houses on the snow during winter.
- **Polar bear awareness:** Polar bears migrate past the village along the sea ice edge. Anyone venturing beyond the town perimeter must carry a rifle and flare gun and understand polar bear defense protocols.
