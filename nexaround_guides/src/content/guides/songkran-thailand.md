## The meaning behind the water

Songkran marks the **traditional Thai New Year**, when the sun moves into Aries. UNESCO describes it as a time for community, forgiveness and family: washing away misfortune, praying for a good year, coming home, and honouring ancestors and elders.

- **Rot nam dam hua**: younger people pour scented water gently over the hands of parents, grandparents and respected elders, and ask for their blessing.
- **At temples**, people make merit, bring food for the monks and bathe Buddha images with scented water. Some build small sand stupas in the temple grounds.
- **Coming home**: many Thais leave the cities to spend the holiday with their families in the provinces.

The water fights grew out of these blessings, and in the big cities they've grown very big indeed.

## Where to go

- **Chiang Mai**: the most famous Songkran in Thailand. The water fight runs around the **moat of the old city**, especially near **Tha Phae Gate**, with people scooping water straight from the moat. Temples such as **Wat Phra Singh** hold processions and bathe Buddha images.
- **Bangkok**: **Silom Road** and **Khao San Road** become water-fight zones, with music and huge crowds. The rest of the city is quieter than usual as people go home.
- Some places start earlier or carry on later than 13 to 15 April, so check locally if your dates are flexible.

## Staying safe

- **Roads**: Thailand has a high number of road accidents, especially involving motorcycles, and the holiday traffic makes it worse. Don't ride a motorbike during Songkran, and never throw water at people on motorbikes or at drivers.
- **Waterproof everything**: phone pouch, a dry bag for cash and passport, or leave them at your hotel. Wear clothes that dry fast and shoes with grip.
- **Ice water and dirty water**: some people use iced water or moat water. Keep your mouth closed when you get hit, and wash afterwards.
- **Respect who isn't playing**: don't throw water at monks, the elderly, babies or anyone who clearly isn't joining in.
- **Alcohol**: streets get rowdy late in the day. Many people prefer the mornings.

## Planning

Flights, trains and buses out of Bangkok are packed just before Songkran and coming back afterwards; book early. Hotels in Chiang Mai fill up for the festival. Some shops and offices close for the holidays, though tourist areas stay open. The UK Foreign Office advises against all but essential travel to Thailand's four southern provinces on the Malaysian border, far from Bangkok and Chiang Mai.
