## What makes it unique

- **The altitude.** The town sprawls between about 4,900 and 5,100 m on the side of Mount Ananea. Nowhere else this high has a town of this size.
- **The cold.** The average temperature over a year is just **1.3 °C**. Nights are below freezing all year, falling to about −12 °C in June.
- **The gold.** Almost everyone here is connected to artisanal gold mining, legal and illegal. Under the traditional *cachorreo* system, miners work about 30 days without pay, then get one day to keep whatever ore they can carry out. Whether it holds any gold is pure luck.
- **The glacier.** The town sits at the foot of the **Auchita glacier**, known as **La Bella Durmiente**, "the Sleeping Beauty".

## The hard truth

**There's no tourist industry here, and the town isn't built for visitors.** Before considering a visit, understand that:

- **There's no plumbing or sewage system**, rubbish piles up, and **mercury** used in gold processing has contaminated drinking water.
- **Health care is minimal**: a single small health post.
- **Crime is a serious problem.** Peruvians call it *la ciudad sin ley*, "the lawless city". Investigations have found widespread **human trafficking**, with women deceived into coming to work in the town's many bars.
- **Altitude is dangerous.** Researchers estimate that at least a quarter of residents suffer from chronic hypoxia. For a visitor, coming up from sea level, sleeping at 5,000 m without weeks of acclimatisation risks **acute mountain sickness, high-altitude pulmonary oedema and cerebral oedema**, all of which can kill.

## If you still want to understand it

- **Don't go alone, and don't go independently.** Only consider it with an experienced, reputable local guide based in Juliaca or Puno, on a daytime visit.
- **Acclimatise properly first:** at least several days around Puno and Lake Titicaca (about 3,800 m) before going higher. Don't sleep in La Rinconada.
- **Respect the people who live there.** These are workers in difficult, dangerous conditions, not a spectacle. Ask before any photograph, and accept a no.
- **Stay out of the bars and nightlife entirely.** Given what's known about trafficking, any money spent there may fund exploitation.

For most travellers, the honest advice is: learn about La Rinconada, but don't go. The Andes around Puno and Lake Titicaca offer high-altitude villages and landscapes that welcome visitors safely.

## How people get there

The nearest airport is at **Juliaca** (3,825 m). From there it's **164 km by road**, about 3½ hours, via Putina and Ananea, climbing all the way. Shared minibuses reportedly carry miners up from Juliaca; a private vehicle with a driver who knows the town is the only sensible option for an outsider.

## When

The dry season, roughly **May to September**, has clearer skies but the coldest nights. Summer (December to March) is rainy, with snow at this altitude.
