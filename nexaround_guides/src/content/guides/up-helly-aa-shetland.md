## A Viking festival from the 1880s

Up Helly Aa looks ancient, but it grew out of a rowdier Victorian custom: young men dragging burning tar barrels through Lerwick at Yule. When that was stopped in the 1870s, torch processions took its place, and in **1889** the first galley was built and burned. Each year a **Guizer Jarl** is chosen to lead the festival as a Norse hero, after years of service on the organising committee.

Since **2023**, women and girls have been able to join the Lerwick squads. Shetland has **eleven** Up Helly Aa festivals in all; Lerwick's is by far the biggest.

## The day

On festival day, **Tuesday 26 January 2027**:

- **From 06:00:** the **Bill**, the Jarl's mock-heroic proclamation, is posted at the **Market Cross**. It stays up until 17:00.
- **From 08:15:** the **Jarl Squad**, in full Viking dress, marches through town to the British Legion, then along the Esplanade and Commercial Street, pausing at the Bill. By about 10:30 they reach the **Bressay ferry terminal**, where the galley stands for photos until 18:00.
- **During the day** the squad visits schools, hospitals and care homes, and the Shetland Museum in the afternoon.
- **19:30:** the torches are lit at the **Hillhead** below the Town Hall, and the procession sets off through the streets.
- **The burning:** the guizers circle the galley at the **King George V Playing Field**, sing the traditional galley song, and throw their torches in. The public can't enter the burning site; watch from the surrounding streets.
- **From 21:15:** the squads tour the town's halls, performing a short act at each, until well into the next morning. The hall parties are private, by ticket or invitation; the Up Helly Aa website sells tickets for the Town Hall. The night after is the "Hop Night".

## Where to watch

For the procession, find a spot on the route early: the streets around the **Town Hall**, **St Olaf Street** and **King Harald Street** fill up long before 19:30. For the burning, the streets overlooking the **King George V Playing Field** give the best view. Dress for a long wait in a Shetland January night: it is usually cold, wet and windy.

## Getting there

- **By sea:** NorthLink ferries sail overnight from **Aberdeen** to **Lerwick** (Holmsgarth terminal), arriving in the morning.
- **By air:** flights land at **Sumburgh Airport**, 41 km south of Lerwick, about 35 minutes by road.
- **Where to stay:** beds in Lerwick sell out months ahead for Up Helly Aa. Book as soon as you can, or look at self-catering houses outside town.

The other ten festivals around the islands are smaller and more local; if you can't get a bed for Lerwick, ask Promote Shetland about their dates.
