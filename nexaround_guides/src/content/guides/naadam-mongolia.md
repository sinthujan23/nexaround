## The three sports

1. **Bökh (Mongolian Wrestling):**
   512 or 1024 wrestlers compete in single-elimination rounds with no weight classes and no time limits. A wrestler loses the moment any part of their body above the knee touches the turf. Competitors wear traditional *zodog* (tight vests) and *shuudag* (briefs) and perform the eagle dance (*devekh*) before and after each bout.
2. **Mori Uldaldaan (Cross-Country Horse Racing):**
   Unlike Western circular track racing, Mongolian races cover 15 to 30 km across rolling grassland terrain. The horses are trained for stamina, and the jockeys are children aged 7 to 12. Watching hundreds of horses charge over the crest of the steppe towards the finish line is unforgettable.
3. **Sur Kharvaa (Archery):**
   Men and women shoot composite horn bows at small woven leather cylinders called *sur*. Teams sing melodic calls (*uukhai*) to signal when a target has been hit.

## How to attend

- **Opening Ceremony Tickets:** Tickets for the morning ceremony inside the National Sports Stadium on 11 July are strictly controlled and often sell out through travel agencies months in advance.
- **Steppe Races at Hui Doloon Khudag:** Free to attend! Shuttle buses run regularly from Sükhbaatar Square and the Dragon Bus Terminal to the racegrounds.
- **Eat Khuushuur:** These deep-fried mutton pancakes are the quintessential festival food. Stalls fry hundreds of thousands throughout the week.
