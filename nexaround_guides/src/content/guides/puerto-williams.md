## Why it's special

- **The latitude.** At 54°56′ S, Puerto Williams is further south than Ushuaia. A few hamlets lie further south still, such as Puerto Toro on the same island, but no other town.
- **A town on an island with no road out.** Navarino Island has roads of its own, but none that link it to the mainland. Everything, from fuel to fresh fruit, arrives by plane or ship.
- **The Yagán.** For thousands of years, the Yagán people lived along these channels, travelling by canoe. Their community lives on at Villa Ukika, on the edge of town.
- **The wilderness behind it.** The Dientes de Navarino, the "teeth of Navarino", rise straight behind the town, with one of the southernmost long-distance treks in the world.

The town began in 1953 as a Chilean navy base, first called Puerto Luisa and later renamed after John Williams Wilson, the founder of Fuerte Bulnes. In 2017 the census counted 1,868 people. In 2019 Chile's statistics office changed its definition of a city so that every provincial capital counts as one, and Puerto Williams officially became the southernmost city in the world.

## Getting there

There are three ways in, and none of them is quick.

**By plane.** Aerovías DAP flies small planes from Punta Arenas to Guardiamarina Zañartu airstrip, a flight of about 1 h 15. Seats are few and flights depend on the weather, so book early and leave a spare day at each end.

**By ferry.** The vehicle ferry *Yaghan* sails from Punta Arenas once a week. The trip takes about 32 hours through the Strait of Magellan and the fjords to the Beagle Channel. Passengers sleep in reclining seats or bunks. Check the day and the fare with the operator, TABSA.

**By boat from Ushuaia.** In summer, small boats cross the Beagle Channel from Ushuaia to Puerto Navarino, on the western side of the island. They only go when the weather allows and enough passengers have booked. From Puerto Navarino it's 54 km by road to town, about 2 hours.

## What to do

- **Climb Cerro Bandera.** The flag on the hill above town is the classic view of Puerto Williams and the Beagle Channel. It's also the first climb of the Dientes circuit, so you can turn it into a long day hike.
- **Walk the Dientes de Navarino circuit.** The loop around the Dientes is about 50 km and takes most people 4 to 6 days. There are no huts and no services: you wild camp and carry everything. Even in summer you can meet snow on the passes. Go only with multi-day trekking experience, or with a guide.
- **Visit the Martín Gusinde Anthropological Museum,** for the story of the Yagán and of the island.
- **Walk in Omora Ethnobotanical Park.** About 1,000 ha of sub-Antarctic forest just outside town, known for its "ecotourism with a hand lens": looking closely at the tiny mosses and lichens of the forest floor.

## The Yagán

Cristina Calderón, born in 1928 near Puerto Williams, was the last native speaker of the Yagán language. She lived in Villa Ukika and died in 2022, aged 93. Chile named her a Living Human Treasure. The Yagán community still lives at Villa Ukika: visit with the same respect you would show any neighbourhood, and ask before you take photographs.

## When to go

- **November to March** is summer: the longest days, the Dientes trail open, boats from Ushuaia most likely to run. Average highs are about 15 °C in January.
- **Winter** is cold but rarely extreme: average lows in July are about −2 °C. Snow and short days close the high trails.
- **Any time of year,** the wind and rain can arrive in minutes. Pack waterproofs even for a walk around town.

## Where to stay

Puerto Williams has small guesthouses, hostels and a few hotels. Book ahead for the summer, when trekkers and yachts arrive, and if you are flying in, ask your host about transport from the airstrip.
