## Read this first: where to go

The most famous Timkat celebrations are in **Gondar**, at the pool of **Fasilides' Bath**, and in **Lalibela**. Both towns are in the **Amhara region**, and as of October 2026 the UK Foreign Office **advises against all travel to Amhara**, as well as to Tigray and several other areas. Check your own government's advice and your insurance before you plan anything.

**Addis Ababa itself is not in those areas**, and Timkat is celebrated across the capital, with the biggest gathering at **Jan Meda**. That's the celebration this guide covers.

## What happens

- **Ketera, the eve (18 January in 2027):** in the afternoon, each church's **tabot** leaves its church on a priest's head, wrapped in brocade and shaded by fringed umbrellas, with deacons, choirs, drums and crowds dressed in white. The processions make their way to a pool, river or open ground, where tents are pitched for the tabots.
- **The night:** the faithful keep vigil with prayers, chanting and hymns. The Divine Liturgy is celebrated in the early hours.
- **Timkat (19 January in 2027):** around dawn the water is blessed and sprinkled over the crowd; some people immerse themselves. Later in the morning the tabots set off back to their churches in great processions, with singing, dancing and drumming in the streets.
- **Some tabots** stay out a day longer and return to their churches the following day.

## In Addis Ababa

**Jan Meda**, the old racecourse in the north of the city, about 9 km from Bole airport, is where tabots from many of the city's churches come together. Arrive in the afternoon of the eve to see the processions arrive, or before dawn for the blessing of the water. Smaller processions leave from churches all over the city, and you'll see them on the roads on both days.

## Visiting with respect

- **Wear white or modest, light clothing.** Many Ethiopians wear the white *netela* shawl.
- **Don't push towards the tabot.** It's the most sacred object in the church, and only priests may touch it.
- **Ask before photographing people**, especially priests and women at prayer, and keep flash off during the night vigil.
- **Expect to be splashed** at the blessing of the water, and keep your phone in a waterproof pouch.

## Practical notes

- Timkat is a **public holiday**: schools and most businesses close, and roads near the processions are blocked. Plan to walk.
- **Book your room early**, especially in central Addis.
- Crowds are huge and pickpocketing happens: carry little, and keep it close.
- Addis Ababa sits at about 2,350 m. Nights in January are cold, so bring a warm layer for the vigil.
