## Exploring the valley

The valley is divided into two distinct halves:
1. **Nishi-Iya (West Iya):** The more accessible section, home to the main Iya Kazurabashi, hot spring onsen hotels, and the Peeing Boy cliff viewpoint.
2. **Oku-Iya (East / Deep Iya):** A far wilder region accessed via Route 439 (one of Japan's most notorious mountain roads). Here you will find the Oku-Iya Double Vine Bridges and Nagoro, the famous "Village of Dolls" where life-sized scarecrows outnumber human residents.

## How to visit

- **Driving vs public bus:** Renting a car at Takamatsu Airport or Oboke Station is by far the best way to explore. The roads are single-lane with passing bays (*taihi-sho*). If using public transport, infrequent Shikoku Kotsu buses connect Oboke Station to Kazurabashi during peak spring and autumn seasons.
- **Walking the vine bridge:** Walking across Iya Kazurabashi is an adrenaline rush. The wooden slats are spaced about 15 cm apart, giving you a clear view of the churning river 14 metres directly below your boots. Cables of steel are now woven into the core for safety, but the sensation of swaying wild vines is completely authentic.
