## Why underground?

Coober Pedy gets brutally hot. The average January maximum is **36.7 °C** and the record is **48.3 °C**, while winter nights can drop to freezing. Since the first opal miners arrived early in the 20th century, people have dug their homes into the hillsides. These **dugouts** stay at a steady, comfortable temperature all year without air conditioning.

The name is usually said to come from the Kokatha words *kupa piti*, often translated as "whitefella hole in the ground".

## What to see

- **Underground churches.** The **Serbian Orthodox Church of St Elijah**, carved out of the rock and hung with icons, is the most striking. There's an underground Catholic church too.
- **Old Timers Mine.** A self-guided walk through an old opal mine and dugout home: the safe way to see the inside of a mine.
- **Opal shops and museums.** This is where most of the world's gem-quality opal comes from. Learn the difference between solid opal, doublets and triplets before you buy.
- **Film-set leftovers.** Coober Pedy has stood in for other planets and post-apocalyptic wastelands in films including *Mad Max Beyond Thunderdome* and *Pitch Black*. A spaceship from a film set still sits in town.
- **Sleep underground.** Several hotels and B&Bs offer underground rooms: totally dark, totally silent, and cool.

## The Breakaways

About **32 km north of town**, the **Kanku-Breakaways Conservation Park** is a line of colourful, flat-topped mesas falling away from the plateau. It's at its best at sunrise and sunset. **You need a permit**, sold in town (ask at the visitor centre). A loop drive back to town follows part of the **Dog Fence**, one of the longest fences in the world, built to keep dingoes out of sheep country.

## Noodling, and a warning

**Noodling**, sifting through spoil heaps for chips of opal the miners missed, is a classic Coober Pedy activity, but **only in the designated public noodling area**. The mining fields around town belong to claim holders.

**Never walk around the opal fields on your own.** There are thousands of **unmarked open mine shafts**, some of them tens of metres deep. Stay on roads and paths, and watch children closely. Signs in town say "don't walk backwards", and they mean it.

## Getting there: the Stuart Highway

It's **850 km from Adelaide**, sealed all the way: north to Port Augusta, then up the Stuart Highway past Woomera and the vast salt lakes.

**The fuel gap:** fill up at **Glendambo**; from there it's **about 254 km** to Coober Pedy with no fuel. **Phone the roadhouse before you rely on it**: if Glendambo is closed, the last fuel is at **Pimba**, about **368 km** from Coober Pedy.

## When to go

**April to October**, when days are warm rather than dangerous. In summer, temperatures over 40 °C are common: drive early, carry plenty of water, and don't break down. If you do, stay with your car.
