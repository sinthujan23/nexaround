## Bonhomme and the carnival

**Bonhomme Carnaval** first appeared on 9 January 1955, when the mayor handed him the keys to the city, and he has been the carnival's host ever since. He opens and closes the festival at his **ice palace**, and turns up at the parades and the snow bath.

Major winter carnivals were held in Quebec City from **1894**, on and off, and it has run every year since **1955**. Attendance peaked at up to a million people in the 2000s; in 2018 the carnival counted more than **530,000** visitor-days.

## What to see

- **Bonhomme's Ice Palace**, built of ice blocks, usually near the Parliament Building, and lit up at night. The opening and closing ceremonies happen here, and there are outdoor dance parties.
- **The night parades**, with lit floats, music and Bonhomme himself, along the Grande Allée.
- **The snow bath** (*bain de neige*): a crowd in swimsuits and hats rolls in the snow on the **Plains of Abraham**. Watching is the sane option.
- **Snow sculptures** on the Plains of Abraham, carved by teams for the competition.
- **Ice canoe racing** across the frozen, drifting St Lawrence.
- **Old Quebec**, the walled city, decorated for the festival, with stands selling maple taffy rolled on snow.

## The effigy: what's free and what isn't

Most events on the main carnival site need the **Bonhomme effigy**, a small tag pass sold at kiosks and shops around the city. Some events, including parts of the programme outside the main site, are free. Check the official programme for this year's prices and what the effigy covers.

## Surviving the cold

February in Quebec City is properly cold, often well below −10 °C with wind chill, and the parades are at night.

- **Layers**, a down coat, a hat that covers your ears, mittens, and warm, waterproof boots with good grip: the streets of the old city are steep and icy.
- **Hand warmers** in your pockets, and a thermos.
- Come inside every hour or so; cafés and shops in the old city are used to frozen carnival-goers.

## Getting there and staying

**Quebec City's Jean Lesage airport** is about 25 minutes from the old city by taxi, and VIA Rail trains run from Montreal. Hotels inside the walls book up early for carnival weekends, so reserve as soon as the dates are out, or stay in Saint-Roch or on the Lévis side of the river and take the ferry across.
