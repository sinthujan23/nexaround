## The route

- **Swakopmund to Cape Cross:** up the coast on a salt road past Henties Bay and the wreck of the **Zeila**, lying in the surf just offshore.
- **Cape Cross:** a colony of **Cape fur seals** so large you'll smell it before you see it. There's an entry fee. The cape takes its name from a stone cross set up here by Portuguese navigator Diogo Cão in 1486.
- **Through the park:** enter **Skeleton Coast National Park** at the **Ugab Gate** and drive north on gravel and salt roads past the fishing camp of **Torra Bay**, then turn inland to leave by the **Springbokwasser Gate**.
- **Into Kaokoland:** through **Palmwag** and **Sesfontein**, desert elephant country, to **Opuwo**, the region's main town, and on to **Epupa Falls** on the Kunene River, with Angola on the far bank.

## Park permits and gate times

The southern part of Skeleton Coast National Park is open to self-drivers; the northern half is a wilderness area, closed except on fly-in safaris.

- **Transit** between the Ugab and Springbokwasser gates needs a permit, issued at the gates. A transit permit **doesn't** let you stay at Torra Bay or Terrace Bay; that needs a booking.
- **Gate times are strict.** Gates open in the morning, stop letting people in by mid-afternoon, and close in the early evening. If you're transiting, you must enter and leave the same day. Sources disagree on the exact cut-off times, so **check the current hours** before you go, and arrive at the first gate early.

## Driving it

- **All gravel and salt roads** after Swakopmund. Salt roads are smooth when dry but slippery when the fog makes them damp.
- **Fog** rolls in off the cold Benguela current on many mornings, cutting visibility to almost nothing. Drive slowly with lights on.
- **Fuel:** fill up in Swakopmund and at every chance after that. Carry spare fuel, water and two spare tyres.
- **No signal** on most of the route. Carry a satellite messenger or beacon.

## Wildlife

The dry riverbeds inland are home to **desert-adapted elephants**, as well as lions, giraffes, oryx (gemsbok) and springbok. On the coast, look for **brown hyenas** and **jackals** around the seal colonies. Keep your distance from elephants, and never get between a mother and her calf.

## Visiting the Himba

Kaokoland is home to the **Himba**, semi-nomadic herders known for the red ochre paste the women wear. Many visitors want to meet them; please do it respectfully:

- Go with a **local guide from the community**, so that the money goes to the village.
- **Ask before taking any photograph,** and accept a no.
- Bring useful gifts if your guide suggests it (food staples rather than sweets), and never treat a home as a tourist attraction.

## When to go

The coast is cool and often foggy all year. Inland, Kaokoland is very hot in summer, and summer rains (roughly November to April) can flood the riverbeds and cut roads. Most visitors go in the **dry winter months, May to October**.

## Where to sleep

Book ahead: options are few. There are guesthouses and campsites in **Swakopmund** and **Henties Bay**; **Terrace Bay** resort and the **Torra Bay** campsite inside the park (Torra Bay opens only in the summer holidays); lodges and community campsites at **Palmwag**, **Sesfontein** and **Opuwo**; and riverside camps at **Epupa**.
