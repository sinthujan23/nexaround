## Why it's special

- **The climb.** From the South African border post at about 1,544 m to Sani Top at 2,876 m, the road climbs 1,332 m in roughly 9 km, with gradients up to 1:3. The last stretch is a staircase of hairpins cut into the cliff.
- **A road built for pack animals.** In 1922 the Basutoland government built a 2 m track here for pack animals. In 1949 David Alexander started running jeeps between Himeville and Mokhotlong, the first vehicle to reach the top was a war-surplus Willys Jeep, and tourist drives began in 1956.
- **A border on the edge.** The border is at the summit, so the whole pass lies in South Africa. At the top you step straight onto the Lesotho plateau, where the air is thin and the winters are hard.

## The drive, leg by leg

| Leg | Distance | Driving |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Underberg → South African border post | 34 km | about 35 min |
| South African border post → Sani Top | 8 km | 46 min at least |
| Sani Top → Mokhotlong | 52 km | about 1 h 06 |

**The lower road** from Himeville has been rebuilt and tarred in stages since 2006, and the upgrade has long been planned to continue towards the pass. In September 2026 local politicians were still calling for repairs to the road, so expect a mix of new surface, gravel and potholes.

**The pass itself,** above the South African border post, is rough gravel, and South Africa allows only 4x4 vehicles on it. If you don't have a 4x4, or have never driven a steep, loose mountain track, book a 4x4 day tour from Underberg or Himeville: drivers who do the pass every day are the safest way up.

**On the Lesotho side,** the road continues across the plateau to Mokhotlong.

## At the border

- **Hours.** The border controls open at 6:00 and close at 18:00. Plan to be through both posts well before closing: there's nowhere to wait in between.
- **Passports.** You leave South Africa at the bottom and enter Lesotho at the top. Carry your passport and check whether you need a visa for Lesotho, and for re-entering South Africa.
- **Weather.** The pass often closes in bad weather, especially in winter, when snow and ice make the hairpins impassable. Check before you set out.

## At the top

Sani Top has a lodge with a pub on the edge of the escarpment, with a view straight down the pass. From here you can walk out across the plateau, visit nearby Basotho villages with a local guide, or tackle **Thabana Ntlenyana**, at 3,482 m the highest mountain in southern Africa, as a long day's hike.

## When to go

- **Spring and autumn** bring the most settled weather and clear views.
- **Summer** (November to March) is green, with afternoon thunderstorms and mist: go early in the day.
- **Winter** (June to August) brings snow and hard frost, and the most closures. The views can be spectacular, but only go with a driver who knows the road in ice.

## Driving safely

Lesotho's roads are often poor, and animals on the road are a hazard, especially at night. Drive in daylight, take it slowly on gravel, and carry warm clothes and water: even in summer, the temperature at the top can drop fast.
