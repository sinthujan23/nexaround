## Why it's special

- **The stone forest.** Over millions of years, groundwater dissolved the limestone of the Bemaraha plateau along its cracks, leaving needle-like peaks and narrow canyons. The park has two main areas: the **Great Tsingy** to the north and the **Little Tsingy** near Bekopaka.
- **Life in the cracks.** The summit, the slopes and the base of a single limestone needle are each a different habitat, and the park has an exceptionally high number of species found nowhere else, many living in tiny niches.
- **The river.** The Manambolo River cuts through the plateau in a gorge, and you can explore it by pirogue from Bekopaka.

The national park covers about 723 km², and a strict nature reserve lies next to it. Together they form the World Heritage Site.

## Getting there

**Fly to Morondava** from Antananarivo, or drive there on a long day from the capital. From Morondava, the road north to **Bekopaka**, the village at the park entrance, is about 200 km of dirt, with two river crossings by ferry: the **Tsiribihina at Belo-sur-Tsiribihina**, and the **Manambolo** just before Bekopaka. Expect 8 to 10 hours of driving, in a 4x4. Many trips break the journey with a night at **Kirindy Forest**, and stop at the **Avenue of the Baobabs** near Morondava on the way.

The road is usually passable only from about **May to November**, and the park is reachable from about June. In the rainy season, from December to April, the road and many others in Madagascar become impassable.

There's also limited access from Antsalova, which has flights from Antananarivo or Mahajanga.

## Safety: read this first

The UK government's advice for western Madagascar, checked in October 2026:

- Because of the risk of **violent highway robberies, particularly at night,** it advises using a **recognised tour operator** when travelling in the region between Besalampy and Morombe. The road from Morondava to Bekopaka is inside that region.
- **Armed criminals have boarded boats on the Tsiribihina River** and robbed the passengers. Multi-day river trips down the Tsiribihina are a popular way to reach the Tsingy: ask your operator how they manage this risk.
- **Don't drive at night** anywhere in Madagascar: few roads are lit and many vehicles have no lights.

In practice, almost everyone visits the Tsingy on an organised trip with a driver from a reputable operator, and that's what we recommend.

## In the park

You walk the circuits of the **Great Tsingy** with a park guide, along paths that climb through the pinnacles on ladders, steps and cables, with suspension bridges over the canyons. You'll want a head for heights, closed shoes, gloves, water and sun protection. The **Little Tsingy** is smaller and easier, close to Bekopaka, and a good half-day. A pirogue trip on the **Manambolo gorge** is a quieter way to see the cliffs.

## When to go

- **June to November** is the season: dry, with the roads open. It gets hotter towards the end.
- **December to April** is the rainy season. Roads flood, ferries can stop, and the park is effectively out of reach.
