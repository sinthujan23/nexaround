## The route at a glance

The road runs about 1,206 km from **Puerto Montt** to **Villa O'Higgins**. Roughly:

- **North (Puerto Montt to Chaitén):** fjords and rainforest, broken up by two ferries.
- **Centre (Chaitén to Coyhaique):** Queulat National Park and its hanging glacier, then Coyhaique, the only real town on the route.
- **South (Coyhaique to Villa O'Higgins):** big lakes, big mountains, long stretches of gravel, and very few people.

## The three ferries

To drive the whole road you need three ferries:

1. **Caleta La Arena – Puelche**, about 45 km south of Puerto Montt: about 40 minutes, running round the clock.
2. **Hornopirén – Caleta Gonzalo**: about **5 hours** through the fjords. **Book this one ahead**, especially with a vehicle in summer.
3. **Puerto Yungay – Río Bravo**, south of Cochrane: about 50 minutes, on the way to Villa O'Higgins.

Many people skip the northern ferries by taking a ferry or flight to **Chaitén** or flying into **Balmaceda**, the airport for Coyhaique, and starting from there.

## Paved, then gravel

The northern and central sections are mostly paved now, but much of the south is still **gravel**. Expect dust when it's dry, mud and potholes when it's wet, and loose stones on bends. A normal car can manage in summer; a higher-clearance vehicle is more comfortable. Carry a full-size spare.

## The best stops

- **Pumalín Park**, around Caleta Gonzalo and Chaitén: rainforest trails and the Chaitén volcano, which erupted in 2008.
- **Queulat National Park**: a short trail leads to a viewpoint of the **Ventisquero Colgante**, a glacier hanging off a cliff with waterfalls pouring from it. There's a park entry fee.
- **Coyhaique**: banks, supermarkets, mechanics and the last big fill-up for a while.
- **Cerro Castillo**: the jagged peaks above Villa Cerro Castillo, with day hikes and a multi-day trek.
- **The Marble Caves**, on **Lago General Carrera**, Chile's largest lake: boats and kayaks leave from Puerto Río Tranquilo to sail into caves of swirling blue-and-white marble. Go early on a calm morning; boats may not run in strong wind.
- **Caleta Tortel**: a short side road leads to a village with no streets, just wooden boardwalks and stairs along the shore.
- **Villa O'Higgins**: the end of the road. From here, between November and April, you can cross into Argentina to **El Chaltén** by boat, on foot or on horseback. It takes one to three days.

## A road with a history

The Carretera Austral was built mostly by the Chilean Army, using conscripts, under the military government of Augusto Pinochet. It opened to traffic in 1988, reached Puerto Yungay in 1996, and Villa O'Higgins in 2000; the branch to Caleta Tortel followed in 2003. The road is still informally associated with Pinochet's name, and you may see it on old signs.

## When to go

**November to March**, the southern summer, is the time: longer days, more places open, and less chance of snow on the passes. January and February are the busiest. Whenever you go, expect rain in the north and strong winds in the south, sometimes on the same day.

## Fuel, money and sleep

- **Fill up at every town**, especially at Coyhaique and Cochrane. South of Coyhaique, stations are few and can run out.
- **Bring cash.** Card payment and ATMs are unreliable in the small villages.
- **Sleep** in campsites, cabins (*cabañas*), hostels and family guesthouses. In January and February, book ahead in the popular stops.
