## Read this first: safety advice

As of September 2026, the **UK Foreign Office advises against all travel on the Karakoram Highway between Mansehra and Chilas** via Battagram, Besham, Dasu and Sazin, up to the junction with the N15. Other governments have their own advice; check yours before you book, and check your insurance too.

In summer there's a way round that stretch: from Mansehra, take the **N15 through Naran and over the Babusar Pass**, which rejoins the KKH near Chilas. That's the route measured on this page. The Babusar Pass is high and **only open in summer**, roughly June to September, depending on snow.

Gilgit-Baltistan, including Hunza, isn't covered by that warning, but conditions change. Check again close to your trip.

## The route

| Stretch | What's there |
| --- | --- |
| **Islamabad → Mansehra** | Out of the plains into the hills. |
| **Mansehra → Naran → Babusar Pass** | Up the green Kaghan valley to the high Babusar Pass. |
| **Babusar → Chilas → Gilgit** | Down to the Indus and back onto the KKH. Near the Raikot Bridge and Jaglot, the road gives views of **Nanga Parbat (8,126 m)**; jeeps from Raikot Bridge go up towards Fairy Meadows. |
| **Gilgit → Karimabad (Hunza)** | Past **Rakaposhi (7,788 m)** to the Hunza valley and its old forts. |
| **Karimabad → Sost** | Through the tunnels along **Attabad Lake**, past the **Passu Cones**. |
| **Sost → Khunjerab Pass** | Up through Khunjerab National Park to the border, at about **4,714 m**. |

## Attabad Lake

In January 2010 a huge landslide blocked the Hunza River about 15 km upstream from Karimabad. The river backed up into a long turquoise lake and drowned more than 20 km of the highway. For years traffic crossed by boat, until a new alignment with **five tunnels** opened in September 2015. Today you can drive alongside the lake, stop at the viewpoints, and take a boat out on it.

## Hunza

**Karimabad** is the base for the Hunza valley, with its two old forts, **Baltit** and **Altit**, orchards, and views of Rakaposhi and the peaks around Ultar. Further north, **Gulmit** and **Passu** sit under the jagged **Passu Cones**, and near Passu the famous swaying **Hussaini suspension bridge** crosses the Hunza River.

## The Khunjerab Pass and China

The road ends (for Pakistan) at the **Khunjerab Pass**, on the border with China. Foreigners pay an entry fee for Khunjerab National Park on the way up.

- **To drive up and back** from Sost, no Chinese visa is needed.
- **To cross into China** you need a Chinese visa arranged in advance, and the border is traditionally open to travellers only from **spring to late autumn**; it closes in winter snow. Check the current dates before you plan around it.

The altitude at the pass is about 4,700 m. If you're coming straight up from Hunza, take it slowly, drink plenty, and don't stay long if you feel unwell.

## When to go

**Late spring to early autumn.** Hunza is beautiful in **April** (apricot blossom) and **October** (autumn colours). The Babusar Pass and Khunjerab are only reliably open in summer. **July to September** brings the highest risk of **landslides** on the highway, so build spare days into your plans.

## Getting around

- **Hire a car with a local driver** in Islamabad or Gilgit: the most common way for visitors, and the safest on these roads.
- **Fly Islamabad–Gilgit** to save two days, if the weather lets the plane in: flights are often cancelled.
- **Public buses and minibuses** run between the main towns.
