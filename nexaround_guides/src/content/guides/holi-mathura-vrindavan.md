## Why Braj

In Hindu tradition, **Krishna** grew up in the villages of Braj, around Mathura and Vrindavan, and played Holi with **Radha** and the cowherd girls of **Barsana**. The towns re-enact those stories every year, and pilgrims come from all over India to join in.

## The week of Holi in Braj

Exact dates are set by the temples' calendars each year and can differ by a day between sources, so check close to your trip. The usual order:

- **Lathmar Holi, Barsana** (about a week before Holi): the men of Nandgaon come to Barsana, and the women "beat" them with long bamboo sticks (*lathis*) while they shelter under shields, all in song and showers of colour. It centres on the **Radha Rani temple** and the lanes below it.
- **Lathmar Holi, Nandgaon** (the next day): the return visit. The men of Barsana come to Nandgaon, and this time the women of Nandgaon wield the sticks.
- **Flower Holi, Vrindavan**: at the **Banke Bihari temple**, priests shower the crowd with flower petals instead of powder. It's beautiful and extremely crowded.
- **Holika Dahan** (the eve of Holi): bonfires across the region recall the story of Holika, burned while the devotee Prahlad survived, the victory of good over evil.
- **Holi** (the main day): coloured powder (*gulal*) and water everywhere, in the streets of Mathura and Vrindavan, from morning until about midday.
- **After Holi**: more celebrations go on for days in villages around Braj, such as the Huranga at Dauji.

## Staying safe

Holi in Braj is joyful and wild, and crowds are enormous.

- **Women should go in a group or with a guide**, and stay in the busier, more family-friendly areas. Harassment is a real problem at Holi, and the UK Foreign Office warns that women travellers in India often face verbal and physical harassment.
- **Watch what you eat and drink.** *Bhang*, a cannabis drink, is a Holi tradition; avoid it unless you know what you're getting, and don't accept drinks from strangers.
- **Crowds can crush** at the temples: stay near the edges, know your way out, and avoid the busiest moments with children.
- **Protect yourself from the colour**: oil your skin and hair beforehand, wear old clothes you can throw away, protect your eyes, and keep your phone in a waterproof pouch.
- Colour play usually stops around midday on Holi itself. **Respect people who don't want to play**, and never throw colour at animals.

## Getting there and staying

**Mathura** is on the main railway line from **Delhi** (about two to three hours by train) and on the Yamuna Expressway. **Vrindavan** is 12 km from Mathura, and **Barsana** about 49 km, an hour or so by car. Rooms in Vrindavan and Mathura fill up early for Holi, often at higher prices; book well ahead.
