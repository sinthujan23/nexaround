## The day at the Treaty Grounds

- **Dawn service, from 5 am**, in **Te Whare Rūnanga**, the carved meeting house on the Upper Treaty Grounds. Thousands brave the early start; since 2017, government ministers have cooked a free breakfast for the crowd afterwards.
- **The waka**: the great war canoe **Ngātokimatawhaorua**, built for the 1940 centenary and described as the world's largest ceremonial war canoe, and other canoes are paddled out from the beach, with karakia (prayers) and haka.
- **The festival**: on 6 February the Treaty Grounds' buildings close and the grounds become a **free festival**, with all-day entertainment on the Upper Treaty Grounds and the sports field opposite, more than 150 market stalls selling art, crafts, clothes and food, and activities for children.
- **Speeches and debate**: politicians and iwi (tribal) leaders come to Waitangi in the days around the 6th, and the Treaty's place in New Zealand life is argued over in speeches and, often, protest. That's part of the day too.

## The rest of the year

On ordinary days, a ticket to the Treaty Grounds includes a guided tour, a cultural performance, two museums, the **Treaty House**, **Te Whare Rūnanga** and the waka. If you want to see the museums properly, come on another day as well: on Waitangi Day itself the buildings are closed.

## Visiting with respect

- Waitangi is a place of deep significance, and the day means different things to different people: celebration, remembrance and protest. Listen more than you talk.
- **Follow the hosts' lead** at the dawn service and any pōwhiri (welcome ceremony): stand when others stand, keep quiet during speeches and prayers, and don't eat or drink inside the meeting house.
- **Ask before photographing people**, and put your phone away during the dawn service.

## Getting there and staying

The Treaty Grounds are just over the bridge from **Paihia**, in the Bay of Islands, about three and a half hours' drive north of Auckland. Flights from Auckland land at **Bay of Islands airport** in Kerikeri, 23 km away. Crowds are huge and traffic jams on the day are common, so **stay in Paihia** if you can and walk over, and book your room months ahead.
