## Before you get in

Iceland's free pools work on trust, and they stay open because visitors keep to a few rules:

- **Leave something in the box.** Most of these pools have a donation box or canister. The money pays for cleaning and repairs.
- **Bring a swimsuit.** Bathing naked isn't the local custom when other people are there.
- **No soap or shampoo** in natural pools, and take every bit of rubbish away with you.
- **Test the water first.** Natural hot water changes temperature, and some pools run well above 40 °C.
- **Don't camp or sleep in changing huts.** It has happened, and it is the quickest way to get a free pool closed.

## Drangsnes hot tubs

From Hólmavík, a 32 km detour (about half an hour) along the north shore of Steingrímsfjörður brings you to Drangsnes, where a row of hot tubs sits on the rocks by the sea, in the middle of the village and easy to spot from the road. Locals use them all year. Admission is a donation. The village campsite and outdoor swimming pool are close by.

## Dynjandi: no longer free

Dynjandi, the great tiered waterfall on Arnarfjörður, is still free to look at, but since summer 2024 the Environment Agency charges a **service fee at the car park**: 750 ISK for a private car in 2024, paid online or by scanning a QR code. The fee pays for the paths, the toilets and the rangers. It's worth it, but it's no longer a free stop.

## Reykjafjarðarlaug

In Reykjarfjörður, a small side fjord of Arnarfjörður on road 63, about 20 km from Bíldudalur, a long outdoor pool sits right by the road. Volunteers built it in 1975, it has changing rooms, and it's free, with a donation box. Just above it, almost hidden, is a small natural hot pot. The water is hot all year, but the temperature varies a lot: the natural pot has been measured at around 45 °C, so feel it before you get in.

## Pollurinn, above Tálknafjörður

Just outside the village of Tálknafjörður, on the hillside, Pollurinn is a set of concrete pools built in 1985 and fed by a hot borehole. Take road 617 and then a steep gravel track up the hill. The pools are divided into sections, from about 36 to 40 °C, with a cold pool added more recently, and they look out over the fjord. The local council provides changing rooms and a shower free of charge; leave a donation, and keep well away from nesting birds on the slope.

## Garðar BA 64

At the head of Patreksfjörður, 17 km from the town (road 62, then road 612), the rusting **Garðar BA 64** lies on the shore. Built in Norway in 1912 as the whaler *Globe IV*, it came to Iceland in 1950, was renamed Garðar BA 64 in 1963, and was later run aground here rather than scuttled. It is the oldest steel ship in Iceland. Walk around it, but don't climb inside.

## Látrabjarg

Látrabjarg is a line of sea cliffs about **14 km long and up to 440 m high**, ending at **Bjargtangar**, the westernmost point of Iceland. Puffins, guillemots, razorbills and one of Iceland's largest colonies of kittiwakes nest on the ledges in summer. It's about 60 km from Patreksfjörður, much of it gravel: allow 1 h 20 each way.

We found no fee for parking at Látrabjarg when we checked in October 2026. A new car park is planned at Bjargtangar, so that may change. **Keep back from the edge:** the turf overhangs the drop, and puffin burrows undermine it.

## Rauðasandur

Off the same road, gravel road 614 climbs over the heath beside Skersfjall and drops in steep hairpins to **Rauðasandur**, about 10 km of reddish-gold sand coloured by crushed shells. It's about 33 km (40 minutes) from Patreksfjörður. The little church of Saurbær stands near the beach. The road is steep and narrow, with no guard rails in places, and snow often closes it in winter and spring.

## Hellulaug

On the shore of Vatnsfjörður, by Flókalundur on road 60, **Hellulaug** is a small rock pool right on the beach, about 60 cm deep and 38 °C, filled from a borehole above it. You can't see it from the road: park in the car park above and follow the path down to the sea. It's always open and there's no entrance fee, just a donation canister by the car park. There's no changing hut, and the rocks are slippery. The restaurant at Flókalundur, a few minutes' walk away, has toilets.

## Roads and seasons

Many of these stops are on gravel, and some roads, like 612 and 614, are steep. Check road conditions on **umferdin.is** before each day's drive, especially outside summer. The pools are open all year, but the gravel roads to Rauðasandur and Látrabjarg can close with snow.
