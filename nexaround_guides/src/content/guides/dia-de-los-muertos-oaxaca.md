## The rhythm of the days

- **31 October (Noche de Brujas & Angelitos):** The spirits of deceased children (*los angelitos*) arrive at midnight. Families place sweets, small toys, and cups of warm milk on altars. The evening vigil at the Panteón Viejo de Xoxocotlán begins as families sweep and decorate graves.
- **1 November (Día de Todos los Santos):** Dedicated to adult departed souls. Grand home altars (*ofrendas*) feature mezcal, cigarettes, mole negro, and pan de muerto with small painted candy faces.
- **2 November (Día de los Fieles Difuntos):** The final day of visits, communal cemetery picnics, and lively brass band street parades (*comparsas*) in neighbourhoods like Jalatlaco and San Agustín Etla.

## Visiting cemeteries with respect

- **Ask before photographing people:** Cemeteries are sacred community spaces. Always ask permission before pointing cameras at families gathered by graves.
- **Never step on graves:** Walk only on established stone paths and grass gaps.
- **Purchase flowers from local vendors:** Support local flower growers outside the gates by buying a bouquet of marigolds to leave at an unadorned grave.
