## The route

**Route 389, Quebec (Baie-Comeau to the Labrador border, about 565 km).** North from the St Lawrence past the huge **Manic-5 dam**, through hundreds of kilometres of forest to the mining towns of **Fermont** and, just over the border, **Labrador City**. Parts are still gravel and parts are under reconstruction; some sections close at times, with flashing lights at the start of the road warning you.

**Route 500, Labrador (Labrador City to Happy Valley-Goose Bay, 543 km).** Paved. **Churchill Falls**, a company town built for one of the world's largest hydro stations, is the only stop between, 243 km in.

**Route 510, Labrador (Happy Valley-Goose Bay to the Quebec border near Blanc-Sablon, about 606 km).** Paved since 2022, through the wildest stretch: about **400 km from Happy Valley-Goose Bay to Port Hope Simpson** with no full services, then down the coast past **Mary's Harbour** and **Red Bay** to **L'Anse-au-Clair** and **Blanc-Sablon**.

From Blanc-Sablon, a ferry crosses the Strait of Belle Isle to **St. Barbe** on Newfoundland, so you can make a loop rather than drive back.

## Phone signal and satellite phones

Mobile coverage is **limited** to the towns. Newfoundland and Labrador runs a **satellite phone loan service** for drivers on Routes 500 and 510: pick up a phone at one town and drop it at another. Check the provincial government's website for current pick-up and drop-off points before you set off.

## Fuel and services

- **Fill up at every town:** Labrador City, Churchill Falls, Happy Valley-Goose Bay, Port Hope Simpson.
- The longest gaps are **287 km** (Churchill Falls to Goose Bay) and **about 400 km** (Goose Bay to Port Hope Simpson). Carry spare fuel, food, water and warm clothes.
- Carry a full-size spare tyre, especially for the gravel on Route 389.

## Along the way

- **Manic-5 (Daniel-Johnson Dam):** an enormous multiple-arch concrete dam right beside Route 389.
- **Churchill Falls:** the town exists to run the underground hydroelectric station on the Churchill River.
- **Red Bay:** in the 16th century, Basque whalers came here every summer. The **Red Bay Basque Whaling Station** is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site, with a visitor centre and the remains of the whaling stations.
- **Point Amour Lighthouse:** a tall stone lighthouse above the Strait of Belle Isle near L'Anse-Amour.

## Wildlife

Watch for **moose** on the road, especially at dawn and dusk. They're big enough to write off a car and its passengers. Black bears and caribou live in the region too.

## When to go

**June to September.** Summer brings long days and open services, but also black flies and mosquitoes; carry repellent and a head net. Winter driving here means extreme cold, snowstorms and very short days, and is only for experienced, well-equipped drivers.
