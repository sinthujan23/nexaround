## Pumalín Park: free trails

**Pumalín Douglas Tompkins National Park**, between Caleta Gonzalo and Chaitén, is free to enter, and so are its trails. You pay only to camp: about **CLP 6,000 per person per night** at the park's campsites in 2026. Two of the best walks start right by the Carretera Austral:

- **Los Alerces** (near Caleta Gonzalo): an easy 1.4 km return, 30–40 minutes, through a grove of alerces, the giant, slow-growing conifers of southern Chile.
- **Volcán Chaitén**: 4.4 km return, about 2½ hours, up a steep path through forest killed by the **2008 eruption** to the crater rim, with a view over the new lava dome. Start early, carry water, and turn back if the cloud comes down.

The park's other short trails, Cascadas Escondidas and the Lago Negro walk among them, are free too. Check at the park for closures: trails are shut for maintenance from time to time.

## Queulat: worth paying for

The **hanging glacier** at Queulat National Park, with its waterfalls pouring off a cliff, is not free: there's a park entry fee. If you've time for only one paid stop in the north, make it this one.

## Cuesta del Diablo

About 10 km north of Villa Cerro Castillo, the road drops off the plateau in a set of tight hairpins, the **Cuesta del Diablo**, with the jagged towers of **Cerro Castillo** across the Ibáñez valley. Pull right off the road at the top to take it in; there's room on the bends, but watch for other traffic.

## The Marble Caves: paid

The marble formations on Lago General Carrera are reached by boat or kayak from Puerto Río Tranquilo, and that costs money. Go early on a calm morning.

## Where the Baker meets the Neff

About **13 km south of Puerto Bertrand**, a short, easy path from the Carretera Austral leads to a viewpoint over the **confluence of the Baker and the Neff**: the clear turquoise Baker, Chile's biggest river by volume, and the milky grey glacial Neff run side by side before they mix. Just upstream, the Baker drops over a powerful rapid more than 10 m high. It's free: there's no ticket and no permit. Keep back from the water's edge.

## Caleta Tortel

Down a side road from the Carretera Austral, **Caleta Tortel** is a village with no streets: houses, a church and a few small shops are linked by kilometres of cypress boardwalks and stairs along the shore. Walking them costs nothing. It's about 125 km from Cochrane, so plan it as an overnight stop.

## The free ferry at Puerto Yungay

South of Tortel, the road reaches the Mitchell Fjord at **Puerto Yungay**, a hamlet with a café and a ferry ramp. The crossing to **Río Bravo** takes about **50 minutes** and is **a free public service**: boarding is first come, first served, so get there early in summer. Departures change with the season; check the current timetable before you drive down from Cochrane, about 125 km away, because missing the last boat can mean waiting until the next day.

Another free crossing, of about 10 minutes, carries the side road from La Junta over the Palena river to the fishing village of **Raúl Marín Balmaceda**.

## Mirador La Bandera, Villa O'Higgins

At the end of the road, a trail from the **Plaza de Armas** in Villa O'Higgins climbs about 1.8 km (roughly 500 m up, 1½ hours) to **Mirador La Bandera**, with views over Lago O'Higgins, Lago Cisnes and Lago Ciervo and the glaciers of the Southern Patagonian Ice Field. Fit walkers can carry on up Cerro Santiago, a couple of hours more. It's free, and it's the best view in town.

The southern half of the road is mostly gravel, and fuel stops are far apart. For the full route, the three ferries and the best time to go, see our [Carretera Austral road trip guide](/road-trips/carretera-austral/).
