## Derby Jetty

Derby sits on **King Sound**, where the tides are the biggest in Australia: the range at Derby can reach **11.8 m**. Walk out along the jetty a few hours apart and you'll see the water arrive over the mudflats, or vanish from under the jetty altogether. It's free, there's a café, and people fish from it. Check a tide table and come for a big spring tide.

## The Boab Prison Tree

About 7 km south of Derby, the **Boab Prison Tree** is a huge, hollow boab with a trunk around 14 m round, believed to be about 1,500 years old. The story goes that police used it as a lock-up for Aboriginal prisoners being walked into Derby in the 1890s. Historians doubt the tree itself was ever used as a cell, but the area is part of that history, and the tree is protected under Western Australia's Aboriginal Heritage Act. It's free to visit; stay behind the fence and don't climb inside.

## Mowanjum Art and Culture Centre

Just off the Gibb River Road near Derby, the **Mowanjum Art and Culture Centre** belongs to the community of three Aboriginal peoples united by their belief in the **Wandjina**, the creator spirits painted on rock across the Kimberley. The **gallery is free** (a gold-coin donation is welcome); the museum costs **$5** and opens on weekdays, 9 am to 4 pm. Hours shrink in the wet season, so check before you go.

## Windjana and Bell gorges: paid

The big gorges of the western Gibb, Windjana, Tunnel Creek and Bell among them, are in national and conservation parks: you need a Western Australian **park pass**, and camping is extra. They're worth it.

## Galvans Gorge

West of Mount Barnett, **Galvans Gorge** is about 1 km off the Gibb River Road: a short, easy walk from the car park leads to a pool full of waterlilies, a waterfall and a plunge pool framed by rock, with **Wandjina rock art** on the walls beside it. You can swim. It's a day stop only: there's no camping. We found no fee here when we checked in October 2026.

## Manning Gorge: paid

Manning Gorge, behind the Mount Barnett Roadhouse, charges a fee, paid at the roadhouse.

## The Pentecost River crossing

Near the eastern end of the Gibb, the road fords the wide **Pentecost River** under the red wall of the **Cockburn Ranges**, one of the great views of the Kimberley. Stopping to look costs nothing. **Saltwater crocodiles live in the Pentecost:** never wade in to check the depth, and stay well back from the water's edge.

## Five Rivers Lookout and the Big Crocodile, Wyndham

Where the Gibb meets the Great Northern Highway, turn north for Wyndham. A sealed road climbs **Mount Bastion** above the town to the **Five Rivers Lookout**, with views over the mudflats where the King, Pentecost, Durack, Forrest and Ord rivers run into the **Cambridge Gulf**. Come at sunset.

At the entrance to town, the **Big Crocodile**, an 18 m sculpture of a saltwater crocodile, is the photo everyone takes.

## Kelly's Knob, Kununurra

At the end of the road, **Kelly's Knob** (Thegoowiyeng) is Kununurra's hilltop lookout, a few minutes' drive from the centre up a sealed road and open around the clock. From the lower lookout by the car park, or the top after a steep 1.2 km return walk, you see the town, Lake Kununurra, the green fields of the Ord River irrigation area and the Sleeping Buddha rock. Parking is limited at sunset: be early.

## Before you go

The Gibb opens only in the dry season, roughly May to October, and closes after rain. Check **Main Roads WA** road conditions before each section, and carry spare fuel, water and two spare tyres. For the full route and the fuel gaps, see our [Gibb River Road guide](/road-trips/gibb-river-road/).
